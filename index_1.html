09 · Risk Research/backtest data. Not investment advice.

Risk and discipline

The strategy sells insurance on near-certain outcomes. The failure mode is not being wrong often — it is being undisciplined once.

Loss asymmetry
1 loss ≈ 49 wins
Win at a 98¢ entry+2¢ per contract (Win)
Loss at a 98¢ entry−98¢ per contract (Loss)

At a 98¢ entry, one loss erases ~49 wins — why automation and hard limits are the product.

Max modeled drawdown
−$3,000
Backtest, at 1,000 contracts per game

Drawdown is monitored continuously against a daily loss limit that halts the agent.

Loss-mitigation exit Measured — candle replay of all 665 positions

Stop-loss: sell at ~70¢ as the price drops

Measured across all 665 positions: every losing game had an exit window. Selling as the price falls through ~70¢ recovers ~73% of the position before zero (median 71¢) and lifts total P&L +75% — +$7,522 to +$13,174, net of fees and false exits.

Fast data required: at free-feed timing recovery drops to 17¢ and the rule hurts. Kill switch and drawdown monitoring run alongside, below. Live validation in Phase 2.

MLB is the proving ground, not the product

The playbook — a near-certain late-game state, a maker-executed entry, and faster data than the market — is sport-agnostic. Every vertical below has the three ingredients; each gets the same Phase 1 replay before a dollar of focus.

Candidate verticals Indicative — each validated with its own MLB-style replay
Vertical Decisive late-game state Data & latency angle Season
NFL Two-score lead inside the two-minute warning Official low-latency feeds purchasable; clock-driven states Sep–Feb
NBA Double-digit lead in the final minutes Nightly game volume; official feed latency edge Oct–Jun
NHL Two-goal lead in the final minutes Well-modeled empty-net dynamics; clock-driven Oct–Jun
College football & basketball Same states, wider spreads and thinner books Less efficient pricing — larger modeled edges plausible Seasonal
Golf Multi-stroke lead in the final round Slow shot cadence lowers the speed bar entirely Weekly, year-round
Soccer Two-goal lead deep in second-half stoppage Flagged risk: VAR can cancel goals — a scored state can reverse Year-round

States chosen for their ≈99%-class hold rates; each vertical is replayed before go-live — the same gate MLB passed. Baseball leads for a reason: highest volume, hard to manipulate, officiating under constant scrutiny.

Year-round coverage

MLB runs Apr–Oct; NFL, NBA, and NHL cover Oct–Jun; golf and soccer fill every week between. The seasonal strategy becomes a continuous book.

One system, many adapters

The signal engine, risk manager, kill switch, and execution agent are sport-agnostic. A new vertical is a feed adapter plus a base-rate table — not a rebuild.

Same gate for every sport

No vertical trades until its own historical replay clears the bar MLB cleared: measured win rates, measured market prices, measured fill dynamics.

Disclosures

Methodology in one-liners. Every number in this brief traces to these sources and methods.

  • Game data — MLB Stats API play-by-play, 2015–2026 (2015–2024 win rates; 2025–26 game states and out-of-sample check).
  • Market data — Kalshi public market-data API, KXMLBGAME series, Apr 2025 – Jul 2026; 7,622 markets scanned.
  • Execution model — maker-fill candle replay of resting bids at 9th-inning entry; adverse selection measured from actual traded prices.
  • Fees — official Kalshi schedule (Jul 2026): taker roundup(0.07·C·P·(1−P)), maker roundup(0.0175·C·P·(1−P)), fee + position cost rounded up to a centicent at order level. Headline figures net of these fees; chart series gross as labeled.
  • Latency — evidence measured at 1-minute resolution; the seconds-level gap is unmeasured and is a Phase 2 deliverable.
  • Framing — measured and modeled figures are labeled as such; all forward-looking figures are labeled Illustrative or Target.
